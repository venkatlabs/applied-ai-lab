## Why this is being studied
Amazon was reported at about 64% of the portfolio on 2026-10-26 against a 10% single-stock planning limit (about 6.4×). That figure is **not re-read today** and must be refreshed before any use. Pay, vesting and retirement savings may depend on the same company, which would make the exposure larger than the stock's price risk. The analysis does not depend on a view of the stock.

## Unverified assumptions (each must be confirmed before any alternative is relevant)
1. Current Amazon weight (the 64% figure is dated 2026-10-26).
2. Lot-level cost basis and holding periods (long vs short term).
3. Which Amazon shares sit in taxable vs tax-advantaged accounts.
4. Employer trading-window, blackout and plan-eligibility rules.
5. Other realized gains or losses this tax year.
6. Federal and Washington tax rates and thresholds, and the income bracket.
7. Wash-sale interactions across all accounts.

## Alternatives to discuss with an adviser (not recommendations)
If the assumptions above hold, an adviser might evaluate some mix of the following. None is a default.

- **Pause new purchases.** One option is to direct new money elsewhere while the question is open.
- **Tax-advantaged accounts.** If Amazon is held inside a 401(k) or HSA and the plan permits it, reallocating there typically has no immediate tax effect. Whether this applies, and how large it is (an earlier estimate was about 9 points of the portfolio), is unverified.
- **Vesting RSUs.** Selling shares at vest generally leaves a small gain, subject to the employer's plan rules and trading windows.
- **Loss offsets.** If taxable positions are below cost, realized losses may offset gains; wash-sale rules limit repurchasing the same security within 30 days. Which positions qualify is unverified.
- **Staged taxable sales.** If the main block has a low basis, a large share of each sale would be gain. A schedule could be sized to an annual tax budget chosen by Venkat with an adviser, not to a price view.
- **Pre-arranged sale plan (e.g. Rule 10b5-1).** Whether Venkat is eligible, and whether employer policy allows one, is a question for the employer and adviser.
- **Gifting appreciated shares** to charity, if Venkat already gives, is another alternative an adviser could assess.
